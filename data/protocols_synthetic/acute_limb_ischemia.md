## Acute Limb Ischemia & Compartment Syndrome
1. IF the patient presents with sudden severe extremity pain, pallor, pulselessness, AND paresthesia:
   THEN diagnose acute limb ischemia, establish immediate vascular surgery consultation, and place extremity in dependent position.
2. IF acute arterial occlusion is suspected AND no active internal hemorrhage or absolute contraindications exist:
   THEN administer immediate intravenous unfractionated heparin bolus followed by continuous infusion as formulary-directed.
3. IF bedside handheld arterial Doppler examination demonstrates absent arterial Doppler signals in distal tibial or pedal vessels:
   THEN request emergent vascular intervention, obtain urgent computed tomography angiography of extremities, and keep patient nil-per-os.
4. IF the patient exhibits signs of acute osteofascial compartment syndrome with tense swollen compartment AND pain on passive stretch:
   THEN remove all circumferential casts, splints, or constrictive dressings and consult surgical team for emergent fasciotomy.
5. IF the patient has a documented history of atrial fibrillation, metallic valve, OR recent myocardial infarction:
   THEN suspect cardiac embolic etiology, evaluate for multiple systemic emboli, and initiate continuous cardiac monitoring.
6. IF the affected extremity demonstrates fixed non-blanching mottling, complete motor paralysis, AND absent sensation:
   THEN recognize irreversible tissue necrosis, avoid futile rapid revascularization, and evaluate for definitive surgical amputation.
7. IF the patient experiences agonizing ischemic extremity rest pain unresponsive to standard non-opioid analgesia:
   THEN administer titrated intravenous opioid fentanyl or morphine as formulary-directed while completing urgent preoperative workup. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF the patient has a functioning infrainguinal prosthetic or venous bypass graft presenting with sudden occlusion:
   THEN alert primary vascular surgeon, administer full anticoagulation as formulary-directed, and prepare for catheter-directed thrombolysis.
9. IF acute phlegmasia cerulea dolens is suspected with massive painful bluish swelling of lower extremity:
   THEN diagnose extensive iliofemoral venous thrombosis, elevate lower extremity, administer intravenous heparin, and consult vascular surgery.
