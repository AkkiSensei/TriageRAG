## Febrile Young Infant Risk Stratification
1. IF an infant aged under twenty-eight days presents with documented rectal temperature elevated above fever threshold:
   THEN admit to hospital, perform complete sepsis evaluation including blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid, and start antibiotics.
2. IF the febrile infant aged twenty-nine to sixty days appears clinically toxic with lethargy, poor perfusion, OR respiratory distress:
   THEN perform full invasive sepsis evaluation, secure vascular access, and administer parenteral antimicrobial therapy as formulary-directed.
3. IF the febrile infant aged twenty-nine to sixty days appears well and meets all validated low-risk clinical criteria:
   THEN obtain complete blood count, catheterized urinalysis, and consider shared decision-making regarding lumbar puncture.
4. IF catheterized urinalysis is positive for leukocyte esterase or nitrites in a febrile young infant:
   THEN diagnose suspected urinary tract infection, send urine culture, and initiate targeted parenteral antimicrobial therapy.
5. IF the febrile infant presents with a simple febrile convulsion lasting under fifteen minutes that has fully ceased:
   THEN reassure parents, search for the underlying infectious source of fever, and avoid routine antiepileptic therapy.
6. IF the febrile infant presents with complex febrile seizure lasting over fifteen minutes or with focal features:
   THEN manage active convulsions with benzodiazepine if ongoing, perform full neurological evaluation, and admit for observation.
7. IF the infant displays bulging anterior fontanelle, neck stiffness, or extreme irritability upon handling:
   THEN suspect acute bacterial meningitis, obtain blood cultures, and administer intravenous ceftriaxone or cefotaxime as formulary-directed.
8. IF fever is accompanied by marked respiratory distress, wheezing, and crackles in an infant during bronchiolitis season:
   THEN obtain viral respiratory panel, provide nasal suctioning and supplemental oxygen if hypoxic, and avoid unnecessary antibiotics.
9. IF the infant displays high fever with petechial or non-blanching purpuric skin lesions:
   THEN suspect invasive meningococcal or pneumococcal disease and administer immediate parenteral third-generation cephalosporin.
