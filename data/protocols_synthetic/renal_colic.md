# Emergency Protocol: Acute Renal Colic & Urolithiasis (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Renal Colic & Urolithiasis
1. IF the patient presents with sudden severe unilateral flank pain radiating to the groin with microscopic hematuria:
   THEN diagnose suspected acute renal colic, establish intravenous access, and provide rapid multimodal analgesia.
2. IF the patient has severe acute renal colic pain AND no contraindications to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs:
   THEN administer intravenous ketorolac or diclofenac as formulary-directed as first-line analgesic therapy.
3. IF the patient has chronic kidney disease, acute renal impairment, OR active peptic ulcer disease:
   THEN withhold non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and administer intravenous opioid morphine or fentanyl as formulary-directed.
4. IF acute renal colic is accompanied by fever, chills, AND systemic toxicity:
   THEN suspect obstructive pyelonephritis with infected stone, obtain emergent blood and urine cultures, and start antibiotics.
5. IF infected obstructed renal stone is confirmed on non-contrast computed tomography of kidneys, ureters, and bladder:
   THEN consult urology immediately for emergency percutaneous nephrostomy or retrograde ureteral stent placement.
6. IF the patient with renal colic has a solitary functioning kidney or bilateral obstructing calculi with anuria:
   THEN request emergency urological decompression to prevent irreversible post-renal acute renal failure.
7. IF severe nausea and retching accompany acute renal colic pain:
   THEN administer intravenous ondansetron or metoclopramide as formulary-directed to control emetic symptoms.
8. IF non-contrast computed tomography reveals an uncomplicated distal ureteral stone measuring under six millimeters:
   THEN initiate medical expulsive therapy with oral tamsulosin as formulary-directed and arrange outpatient urology clinic review.
9. IF the patient achieves complete pain relief with oral analgesics, has normal vital signs, AND no infected stone:
   THEN discharge home with oral analgesics, strain urine for stone collection, and provide clear red-flag return precautions.
