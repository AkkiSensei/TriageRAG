## Diabetic Ketoacidosis & Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar State

   THEN obtain immediate arterial blood gas, electrolyte panel, and initiate intravenous isotonic crystalloid fluid resuscitation.
2. IF serum potassium is below threshold prior to planned insulin initiation in diabetic ketoacidosis:
   THEN withhold insulin therapy completely and administer intravenous potassium replacement as formulary-directed until potassium normalizes.
3. IF serum potassium is documented within normal or elevated range AND fluid resuscitation is underway:
   THEN initiate continuous intravenous regular insulin infusion as formulary-directed with hourly capillary glucose monitoring.
4. IF blood glucose drops below target ceiling while anion gap remains elevated in diabetic ketoacidosis:
   THEN add five percent dextrose to intravenous fluids as formulary-directed to permit continued insulin infusion for ketoacidosis clearance.
5. IF the patient exhibits extreme hyperglycemia with severe hyperosmolality AND absent significant ketoacidosis:
   THEN diagnose hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state, administer gradual fluid resuscitation, and avoid overly rapid osmolality reduction.
6. IF the patient exhibits profound dehydration, volume contraction, and metabolic acidemia secondary to osmotic diuresis:
   THEN administer initial rapid intravenous normal saline bolus as formulary-directed before switching to maintenance fluids.
7. IF severe arterial acidemia with blood pH critically depressed is documented on arterial blood gas:
   THEN evaluate for concurrent lactic acidosis or sepsis and consider cautious isotonic sodium bicarbonate as formulary-directed. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF the patient exhibits sudden headache, declining level of consciousness, and bradycardia during treatment:
   THEN suspect acute cerebral edema, administer intravenous mannitol or hypertonic saline as formulary-directed, and slow fluids.
9. IF hypokalemia develops during insulin therapy as potassium shifts intracellularly:
   THEN add potassium chloride to all subsequent intravenous fluid bags as formulary-directed to maintain potassium within target.
