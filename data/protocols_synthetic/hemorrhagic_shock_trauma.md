## Hemorrhagic Shock & Damage Control Resuscitation
1. IF massive transfusion is required in severe trauma with hemodynamic instability unresponsive to initial blood products:
   THEN activate institutional massive transfusion protocol delivering packed red blood cells, plasma, and platelets in equal ratio.
2. IF trauma-induced coagulopathy with hyperfibrinolysis is documented on viscoelastic rotational thromboelastometry:
   THEN administer intravenous cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate as formulary-directed to restore hemostasis.
3. IF pericardial tamponade is identified on extended focused assessment with sonography for trauma with hypotension and distended veins:
   THEN perform immediate emergency ultrasound-guided pericardiocentesis or resuscitative thoracotomy if arrest is imminent.
4. IF hemoperitoneum is confirmed on ultrasound with persistent hemodynamic instability unresponsive to blood resuscitation:
   THEN transport patient directly to operating theater for emergency damage-control exploratory laparotomy.
5. IF severe hypothermia develops during trauma resuscitation with core body temperature below threshold:
   THEN apply active internal and external rewarming, use blood fluid warmers, and maintain ambient operating room temperature.
6. IF severe metabolic acidosis with base deficit widening occurs in lethal triad of trauma resuscitation:
   THEN prioritize surgical and interventional hemorrhage control over excessive crystalloid fluid infusion to arrest shock cycle.
7. IF the patient suffers traumatic cardiac arrest within ten minutes of penetrating thoracic trauma:
   THEN perform immediate emergency department thoracotomy, cross-clamp descending thoracic aorta, and relieve tamponade.
8. IF severe pelvic fracture hemorrhage is refractory to pelvic binder application and massive transfusion:
   THEN consult interventional radiology for urgent pelvic angioembolization or perform retroperitoneal pelvic packing.
9. IF hemodynamic stability is achieved with permissive hypotension systolic target maintained until surgical hemostasis:
   THEN avoid excessive crystalloid fluids to prevent clot disruption and hemodilution until definitive surgical control achieved.
