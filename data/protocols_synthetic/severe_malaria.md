## Severe Falciparum Malaria Management
1. IF the patient presents with confirmed or suspected malaria AND altered consciousness or coma:
   THEN diagnose cerebral malaria, secure airway, obtain blood parasitemia smear, and start intravenous artesunate immediately.
2. IF severe malaria is confirmed with hyperparasitemia, severe anemia, OR acute organ dysfunction:
   THEN administer intravenous artesunate as formulary-directed at zero, twelve, and twenty-four hours.
3. IF intravenous artesunate is unavailable in the emergency treatment facility:
   THEN administer intramuscular artemether or intravenous quinine infusion as formulary-directed under continuous cardiac monitoring.
4. IF the patient with severe malaria exhibits capillary blood glucose below threshold:
   THEN administer immediate intravenous concentrated dextrose bolus as formulary-directed and monitor glucose hourly.
5. IF acute respiratory distress with non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema develops in severe malaria:
   THEN avoid excessive fluid administration, apply positive pressure ventilation, and consider hemodialysis for volume control.
6. IF acute kidney injury with oliguria and elevated creatinine develops in severe falciparum infection:
   THEN initiate renal replacement therapy and adjust concurrent medication dosages for impaired clearance.
7. IF severe malarial anemia with hemoglobin critically depressed AND hemodynamic compromise is present:
   THEN administer emergency transfusion of screened packed red blood cells as formulary-directed.
8. IF spontaneous mucosal bleeding or disseminated intravascular coagulation is identified:
   THEN administer fresh frozen plasma and platelets as formulary-directed for active clinical hemorrhage.
9. IF black tea-colored urine with intravascular hemolysis (blackwater fever) is observed:
   THEN maintain adequate hydration, monitor renal function, and consult nephrology for potential dialysis.
