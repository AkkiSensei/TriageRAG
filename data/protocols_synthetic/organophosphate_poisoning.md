## Acute Organophosphate & Carbamate Toxicity
1. IF the patient presents with acute organophosphate exposure, pinpoint pupils, diaphoresis, AND severe bronchorrhea:
   THEN remove all contaminated clothing, decontaminate skin with soap and water, and wear full personal protective equipment.
2. IF the patient exhibits cholinergic crisis with copious bronchial secretions, wheezing, AND bradycardia:
   THEN administer intravenous atropine as formulary-directed immediately and double the dose every three to five minutes until secretions dry.
3. IF adequate atropinization criteria are fulfilled including clear chest auscultation, heart rate normalized, AND dry axillae:
   THEN maintain continuous intravenous atropine infusion as formulary-directed to prevent recrudescence of pulmonary secretions.
4. IF the patient has confirmed organophosphate toxicity with prominent neuromuscular weakness or fasciculations:
   THEN administer intravenous pralidoxime as formulary-directed as an initial bolus followed by continuous infusion.
5. IF the patient presents with pure carbamate poisoning rather than organophosphate exposure:
   THEN administer intravenous atropine as formulary-directed for secretions and withhold pralidoxime as carbamylation is spontaneously reversible.
6. IF seizure activity or severe agitation occurs during acute organophosphate poisoning:
   THEN administer intravenous diazepam or midazolam as formulary-directed for central nervous system protection and seizure termination.
7. IF severe respiratory muscle paralysis or depressed level of consciousness impairs spontaneous ventilation:
   THEN proceed with emergency endotracheal intubation using non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockers where possible. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF succinylcholine is considered for rapid sequence intubation in organophosphate toxicity:
   THEN withhold succinylcholine completely as pseudocholinesterase inhibition causes prolonged, life-threatening paralysis.
9. IF hypotension develops secondary to excessive vasodilation and bradycardia despite initial atropine dosing:
   THEN continue aggressive atropine titration and administer intravenous crystalloid bolus as formulary-directed.
