## Cholinergic Crisis & Intermediate Syndrome Escalation
1. IF pulmonary bronchorrhea recurs during downward titration of intravenous atropine infusion:
   THEN re-bolus intravenous atropine and increase maintenance infusion rate to re-establish dried pulmonary airways.
2. IF the patient develops atropine toxicity with hyperthermia, flushed dry skin, delirium, AND absent bowel sounds:
   THEN withhold atropine temporarily until toxic anticholinergic signs subside, then resume at reduced maintenance rate.
3. IF the patient develops intermediate syndrome with weakness of neck flexors, cranial nerves, AND respiratory muscles:
   THEN provide prolonged mechanical ventilatory support, maintain diligent pulmonary hygiene, and provide supportive care.
4. IF delayed organophosphate-induced polyneuropathy develops two to four weeks post-exposure with distal motor weakness:
   THEN provide physical therapy, orthotic devices, and supportive rehabilitation without repeat oxime therapy.
5. IF the patient has co-ingested hydrocarbon solvent vehicle resulting in chemical pneumonitis and hypoxia:
   THEN withhold gastric lavage, avoid inducing emesis, apply positive pressure ventilation, and treat hypoxemia.
6. IF severe refractory ventricular arrhythmias such as torsades de pointes develop secondary to QT prolongation:
   THEN administer intravenous magnesium sulfate as formulary-directed and correct concurrent electrolyte disturbances.
7. IF aspiration of gastric contents occurs during initial decontamination or seizure activity:
   THEN perform deep tracheal suctioning, provide supplemental oxygen, and administer empirical antibiotics if infection develops.
8. IF the patient remains fully stable with dry secretions, clear lungs, and normal pupils twenty-four hours after atropine cessation:
   THEN transfer to inpatient medical floor, arrange psychiatric evaluation for intentional ingestion, and observe closely.
9. IF persistent tachycardia is present in a patient whose lungs are clear of bronchial secretions:
   THEN do not treat tachycardia with beta-blockers as tachycardia is an expected atropine effect and bronchorrhea is the primary target.
