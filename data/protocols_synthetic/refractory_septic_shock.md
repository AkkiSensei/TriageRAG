# Emergency Protocol: Refractory Septic Shock & Multi-Organ Failure Escalation (SYNTHETIC)

## Refractory Septic Shock & Multi-Organ Failure Escalation
1. IF septic shock is accompanied by severe metabolic lactic acidosis with blood pH critically depressed:
   THEN optimize tissue perfusion with vasopressors and consider continuous veno-venous hemofiltration for acid clearance.
2. IF dynamic fluid responsiveness testing indicates fluid unresponsiveness with elevated stroke volume variation:
   THEN stop crystalloid fluid administration to prevent worsening pulmonary oedema and tissue congestion.
3. IF severe thrombocytopenia and disseminated intravascular coagulation with spontaneous bleeding develop:
   THEN administer platelets, fresh frozen plasma, or cryoprecipitate as formulary-directed for active clinical bleeding.
4. IF acute oliguric renal failure with severe hyperkalemia complicates septic shock resuscitation:
   THEN initiate emergency renal replacement therapy and adjust antimicrobial dosing for impaired renal clearance.
5. IF refractory vasoplegic shock persists despite high-dose norepinephrine and vasopressin infusions:
   THEN consider third-line intravenous angiotensin II or methylene blue as formulary-directed in intensive care.
6. IF the patient exhibits signs of severe hypoxemic respiratory failure AND refractory shock:
   THEN evaluate for veno-venous or veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation rescue therapy.
7. IF profound hypothermia with core body temperature below threshold is present in severe sepsis:
   THEN apply active external warming blankets and warm intravenous fluids to restore core normothermia.
8. IF the primary site of infection is confirmed as central venous catheter-related bloodstream infection:
   THEN remove offending central venous catheter immediately and insert new vascular access at an alternate anatomical site.
9. IF clinical stabilisation is achieved with mean arterial pressure sustained above target and lactate clearance:
   THEN begin systematic downward titration of vasopressors, weaning second-line agents prior to primary norepinephrine.
