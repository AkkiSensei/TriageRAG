# Emergency Protocol: Major Trauma Primary Survey & Resuscitation (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Major Trauma Primary Survey & Resuscitation
1. IF the trauma patient arrives with potential spinal injury following high-energy blunt trauma mechanism:
   THEN enforce manual in-line cervical spine stabilization, assess airway patency, and avoid cervical spine movement.
2. IF catastrophic external hemorrhage is identified from an extremity during initial trauma assessment:
   THEN apply immediate direct pressure and place a commercial arterial tourniquet proximal to wound if bleeding uncontrolled.
3. IF the trauma patient presents with snoring, gurgling, or foreign body airway obstruction:
   THEN perform jaw-thrust maneuver, clear oral secretions with suction, and insert oropharyngeal or nasopharyngeal airway.
4. IF airway compromise, severe facial trauma, or GCS score below eight is identified in major trauma:
   THEN perform rapid sequence endotracheal intubation with manual in-line cervical spine stabilization.
5. IF the patient exhibits severe respiratory distress, unilateral absent breath sounds, AND hypotension:
   THEN diagnose tension pneumothorax and perform immediate needle or finger thoracostomy in fifth intercostal space midaxillary line.
6. IF an open sucking chest wound is identified on physical examination of the thorax:
   THEN apply a three-sided vented chest seal and perform tube thoracostomy through a separate incision.
7. IF massive hemothorax is identified with dullness to percussion AND over one thousand milliliters of blood on chest drainage:
   THEN initiate blood transfusion, collect shed blood for autotransfusion, assess for systemic hypoperfusion, and consult cardiothoracic surgery for urgent thoracotomy.
8. IF the trauma patient is in hemorrhagic shock with systolic blood pressure below target AND tachycardia:
   THEN administer intravenous tranexamic acid within three hours of injury as formulary-directed and initiate balanced blood transfusion.
9. IF pelvic ring fracture is suspected with pelvic instability, perineal hematoma, OR severe pelvic pain:
   THEN apply commercial pelvic circumferential binder at the level of the greater trochanters and avoid repeat pelvic manipulation.
