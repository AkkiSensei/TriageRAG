# Emergency Protocol: Snake Envenomation & Antivenom Administration (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Snake Envenomation & Antivenom Administration
1. IF the patient presents with suspected snakebite AND visible fang marks, local swelling, OR systemic symptoms:
   THEN immobilize affected limb with a splint at heart level, keep patient calm, and strictly avoid tourniquets or wound incisions.
2. IF the twenty-minute whole blood clotting test is non-clotting OR spontaneous systemic bleeding is present:
   THEN diagnose systemic hemotoxic envenomation and initiate intravenous polyvalent snake antivenom as formulary-directed.
3. IF the patient exhibits progressive neurotoxic symptoms including bilateral ptosis, diplopia, AND bulbar weakness:
   THEN diagnose neurotoxic envenomation, administer intravenous antivenom as formulary-directed, and prepare for airway support.
4. IF respiratory muscle paralysis, shallow breathing, OR hypoxemia develops in neurotoxic snakebite:
   THEN provide immediate bag-valve-mask ventilatory assistance and proceed with definitive endotracheal intubation.
5. IF severe early anaphylactoid reaction develops during antivenom infusion with urticaria, coughing, or hypotension:
   THEN stop antivenom temporarily, administer intramuscular epinephrine as formulary-directed, and resume antivenom at lower rate.
6. IF the neurotoxic envenomation is suspected secondary to elapid cobra bite with prominent neuromuscular junction blockade:
   THEN administer intravenous atropine followed by neostigmine as formulary-directed as anticholinesterase trial.
7. IF extensive local swelling, blistering, and progressive necrosis spread rapidly past major joints:
   THEN monitor limb compartment pressures, elevate limb slightly, and consult general surgery for wound management.
8. IF hypotension and shock develop in hemotoxic viper bite secondary to third-spacing and capillary leak:
   THEN administer rapid intravenous crystalloid bolus as formulary-directed in addition to adequate antivenom dosing.
9. IF the twenty-minute whole blood clotting test remains completely clotted and no neurotoxic or local signs occur after twelve hours:
   THEN continue close clinical observation for twenty-four hours before discharging with wound care instructions.
