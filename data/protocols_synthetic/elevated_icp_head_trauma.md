## Intracranial Hypertension & Cerebral Perfusion Escalation
1. IF intracranial pressure monitoring reveals sustained intracranial pressure elevated above twenty millimeters of mercury:
   THEN elevate head of bed thirty degrees, ensure neutral neck alignment, and administer intravenous hypertonic saline bolus.
2. IF intracranial hypertension remains refractory to first-tier osmotherapy and head elevation:
   THEN administer continuous deep intravenous sedation and neuromuscular blockade as formulary-directed under neurointensive care.
3. IF refractory intracranial hypertension persists despite maximal medical osmotherapy and sedation:
   THEN consult neurosurgery for emergency decompressive hemicraniectomy or external ventricular drain CSF drainage.
4. IF the patient is receiving oral anticoagulant warfarin AND presents with acute traumatic intracranial hemorrhage:
   THEN administer four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate and intravenous vitamin K as formulary-directed for emergent reversal.
5. IF the patient is receiving oral direct factor Xa inhibitors and presents with traumatic intracranial hemorrhage:
   THEN administer specific reversal agent andexanet alfa or four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate as formulary-directed.
6. IF fever is documented in the severe traumatic brain injury patient during intensive care management:
   THEN enforce strict normothermia with antipyretics and surface cooling devices to minimize secondary metabolic cerebral injury.
7. IF severe hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia is detected on routine blood glucose surveillance:
   THEN initiate continuous insulin infusion or dextrose titration as formulary-directed to maintain strict euglycemia.
8. IF paroxysmal sympathetic hyperactivity with tachycardia, hypertension, tachypnea, AND dystonia develops:
   THEN administer oral or enteral propranolol, clonidine, or bromocriptine as formulary-directed for sympathetic storming.
9. IF diabetes insipidus develops following traumatic brain injury with sudden massive polyuria and hypernatremia:
   THEN administer intravenous or subcutaneous desmopressin as formulary-directed and replace urinary free water losses.
