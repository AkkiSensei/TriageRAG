## Acute Stroke Complications & Neurological Deterioration

   THEN consult neurosurgery urgently for decompressive hemicraniectomy assessment and initiate osmotherapy as formulary-directed.
2. IF acute cerebellar infarction produces brainstem compression and obstructive acute hydrocephalus:
   THEN request immediate emergency neurosurgical intervention for suboccipital craniectomy or external ventricular drain placement.
3. IF the acute stroke patient exhibits new-onset generalized motor convulsions during initial emergency management:
   THEN administer intravenous lorazepam as formulary-directed and initiate non-sedating antiepileptic therapy with levetiracetam.
4. IF fever is documented following acute ischemic stroke presentation:
   THEN administer oral or intravenous paracetamol as formulary-directed to enforce strict normothermia and investigate occult infection.
5. IF acute dysphagia is identified on bedside swallow screening examination:
   THEN place patient on strict nil-per-os status to prevent aspiration pneumonia and obtain speech pathology swallow review.
6. IF the patient is ineligible for thrombolytic or endovascular therapy due to delayed presentation beyond time windows:
   THEN initiate oral aspirin as formulary-directed and maintain permissive hypertension to support ischemic penumbral flow.
7. IF hypotension develops in an acute stroke patient with systolic blood pressure below cerebral perfusion target:
   THEN administer intravenous isotonic saline bolus and titrate vasopressor therapy as formulary-directed to maintain penumbra.
8. IF deep vein thrombosis prophylaxis is considered within the first twenty-four hours of thrombolysis:
   THEN withhold chemical thromboprophylaxis for twenty-four hours and apply intermittent pneumatic compression devices.
9. IF acute cardiac troponin elevation or arrhythmia is detected during post-stroke telemetry monitoring:
   THEN obtain 12-lead electrocardiogram, continuous telemetry, and evaluate for neurogenic myocardial stunning or concurrent infarction.
