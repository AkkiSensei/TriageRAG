## Peritonitis & Intra-Abdominal Sepsis Escalation
1. IF intra-abdominal sepsis is confirmed secondary to perforated viscus or complicated intra-abdominal infection:
   THEN administer intravenous piperacillin-tazobactam or carbapenem as formulary-directed covering enteric gram-negative and anaerobic flora.
2. IF the patient develops abdominal compartment syndrome with intra-abdominal pressure elevated AND new oliguria:
   THEN decompress stomach and rectum with tubes, optimize fluid balance, and prepare for emergency surgical abdominal decompression.
3. IF acute appendicitis is suspected with focal right lower quadrant tenderness, fever, AND leukocytosis:
   THEN obtain confirmatory ultrasound or computed tomography scan and prepare for laparoscopic or open appendectomy within twelve hours.
4. IF acute calculous cholecystitis is complicated by high fever, severe jaundice, AND hypotension (acute cholangitis):
   THEN diagnose ascending cholangitis, initiate broad-spectrum antibiotics, and consult gastroenterology for emergent biliary drainage.
5. IF strangulated abdominal wall or groin hernia is identified with severe pain, erythema, and irreducibility:
   THEN forbid forceful manual reduction, maintain nil-per-os status, and arrange immediate emergency operative exploration.
6. IF extensive intra-abdominal hemorrhage occurs following blunt or penetrating trauma with positive FAST ultrasound:
   THEN activate massive transfusion protocol, administer tranexamic acid as formulary-directed, and transport to operating suite.
7. IF severe uncompensated metabolic lactic acidosis is present in a patient with suspected ischemic bowel:
   THEN avoid mesenteric vasoconstrictors if possible, optimize fluid volume, and proceed with emergency exploratory laparotomy.
8. IF the patient has a known abdominal aortic aneurysm presenting with sudden back or flank pain AND syncope:
   THEN diagnose ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm, maintain permissive hypotension, and transfer directly to vascular operating room.
9. IF post-operative acute abdomen develops with wound dehiscence or anastomotic leak following recent abdominal surgery:
   THEN administer broad-spectrum antibiotics, obtain urgent contrast imaging, and alert primary surgical team immediately.
