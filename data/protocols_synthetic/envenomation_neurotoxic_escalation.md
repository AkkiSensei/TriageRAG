## Refractory Envenomation & Coagulopathy Escalation
1. IF the twenty-minute whole blood clotting test remains non-clotting six hours after initial antivenom completion:
   THEN administer repeat dose of intravenous polyvalent snake antivenom as formulary-directed and re-check clotting test.
2. IF severe acute kidney injury with oliguria and dark tea-colored urine develops following viper envenomation:
   THEN maintain adequate renal perfusion, avoid nephrotoxic drugs, and initiate hemodialysis for refractory uremia or hyperkalemia.
3. IF compartment syndrome is clinically suspected with severe pain on passive stretch, tense compartment, AND paresthesias:
   THEN measure direct compartment pressure and ensure full antivenom-induced coagulopathy correction prior to any surgical fasciotomy.
4. IF secondary bacterial cellulitis or abscess develops at the bite site forty-eight hours post-envenomation:
   THEN obtain wound swab for microbiological culture and initiate targeted broad-spectrum oral or parenteral antibiotics.
5. IF severe delayed serum sickness develops five to twelve days after antivenom therapy with fever and arthralgias:
   THEN administer a short course of oral antihistamines and corticosteroids as formulary-directed.
6. IF severe local tissue necrosis with extensive non-viable skin and subcutaneous tissue is demarcated:
   THEN arrange delayed surgical debridement and reconstructive skin grafting after acute envenomation resolves.
7. IF the patient develops persistent profound hypotension despite repeated antivenom and crystalloid volume resuscitation:
   THEN initiate continuous intravenous norepinephrine infusion as formulary-directed and monitor invasive arterial pressure.
8. IF anticholinesterase test with neostigmine demonstrates clear improvement in ptosis and vital capacity:
   THEN continue maintenance doses of intravenous or intramuscular neostigmine with atropine as formulary-directed.
9. IF active bleeding persists despite repeat antivenom and normalization of coagulation parameters:
   THEN administer fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate, or packed red blood cells as formulary-directed for volume support.
