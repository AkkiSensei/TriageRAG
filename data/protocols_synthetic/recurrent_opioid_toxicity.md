2. IF the patient develops severe chest wall rigidity (wooden chest syndrome) preventing bag-valve-mask ventilation:
   THEN administer rapid-acting neuromuscular blocking agent and proceed with immediate endotracheal airway intubation.
3. IF profound hypothermia with subnormal core temperature accompanies prolonged unconsciousness in opioid overdose:
   THEN initiate active core rewarming with warm fluids and blankets and monitor continuous core body temperature.
4. IF rhabdomyolysis and acute kidney injury develop following prolonged immobility and coma:
   THEN initiate aggressive intravenous crystalloid hydration, check serum creatine kinase, and monitor urine output closely.
5. IF the patient aspirates gastric contents during period of depressed consciousness prior to arrival:
   THEN perform deep endotracheal suctioning, apply lung-protective ventilation, and monitor for acute lung injury.
6. IF hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy is suspected with persistent coma despite full reversal of opioid toxidrome:
   THEN perform non-contrast cranial computed tomography scan and transfer to intensive care unit for targeted neuroprotection.
7. IF the patient is an intentional body packer with multiple ingested drug packets visible on abdominal imaging:
   THEN obtain immediate surgical and toxicological consultation, enforce continuous telemetry, and avoid oil-based laxatives.
8. IF an ingested drug packet ruptures producing sudden massive overdose, seizures, AND circulatory collapse:
   THEN perform emergency endotracheal intubation, initiate high-dose naloxone infusion, and transfer to operating room for laparotomy.
9. IF persistent hypotension with vasodilation accompanies synthetic opioid overdose:
   THEN administer intravenous crystalloid bolus and titrate intravenous norepinephrine as formulary-directed if fluid-unresponsive.
