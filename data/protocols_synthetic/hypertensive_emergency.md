## Acute Hypertensive Emergency with End-Organ Damage
1. IF the patient exhibits severe arterial hypertension with systolic blood pressure severely elevated AND acute chest pain:
   THEN evaluate for acute coronary syndrome or aortic dissection and initiate titratable intravenous labetalol or nicardipine.
2. IF acute aortic dissection is confirmed with severe blood pressure elevation AND pulse deficit:
   THEN rapidly lower heart rate below sixty beats per minute with intravenous beta-blocker before lowering systolic blood pressure.
3. IF the patient exhibits severe hypertension AND acute ischemic stroke eligible for thrombolytic therapy:
   THEN lower blood pressure cautiously below target thrombolysis ceiling using intravenous labetalol or nicardipine as formulary-directed.
4. IF the patient exhibits acute hypertensive encephalopathy with severe headache, confusion, AND visual disturbances:
   THEN reduce mean arterial pressure by no more than twenty-five percent in the first hour to prevent cerebral ischemic injury.
5. IF the patient has acute hypertensive pulmonary oedema with respiratory distress AND rales:
   THEN administer continuous intravenous nitroglycerin infusion as formulary-directed and apply non-invasive ventilation.
6. IF the patient exhibits severe hypertension AND acute renal impairment with oliguria or hematuria:
   THEN initiate intravenous fenoldopam or nicardipine as formulary-directed with close monitoring of urine output and renal function.
7. IF the patient presents with severe hypertension in late pregnancy with proteinuria AND hyperreflexia:
   THEN initiate pre-eclampsia protocol, administer intravenous magnesium sulfate as formulary-directed, and consult obstetrics.
8. IF severe hypertension is accompanied by suspected pheochromocytoma crisis with diaphoresis and tremor:
   THEN administer intravenous phentolamine or alpha-adrenergic blocker as formulary-directed and avoid unapposed beta-blockade.
9. IF the patient has severe blood pressure elevation WITHOUT any clinical evidence of acute end-organ damage:
   THEN withhold aggressive intravenous antihypertensive therapy, initiate oral agents, and arrange close outpatient follow-up.
