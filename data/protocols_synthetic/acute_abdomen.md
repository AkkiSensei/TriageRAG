# Emergency Protocol: Acute Abdomen & Peritoneal Irritation (SYNTHETIC)

## Acute Abdomen & Peritoneal Irritation
1. IF the patient presents with sudden severe abdominal pain, involuntary guarding, AND board-like abdominal rigidity:
   THEN diagnose acute peritonitis, place patient on strict nil-per-os status, initiate intravenous fluids, and consult surgery STAT.
2. IF free intraperitoneal air is identified on erect chest radiograph or abdominal computed tomography scan:
   THEN confirm gastrointestinal tract perforation, administer intravenous broad-spectrum antibiotics, and prepare for emergency laparotomy.
3. IF the patient with acute abdomen exhibits rigid involuntary guarding, peritonitis, AND signs of septic shock:
   THEN administer rapid intravenous crystalloid bolus as formulary-directed, measure lactate, and start vasopressors if refractory.
4. IF the patient is a female of childbearing age presenting with acute lower abdominal or pelvic pain:
   THEN obtain immediate urine or serum pregnancy test to rule out ruptured ectopic pregnancy before radiating imaging.
5. IF ruptured ectopic pregnancy is suspected with positive pregnancy test, severe pelvic pain, AND free fluid on bedside ultrasound:
   THEN activate emergency gynecological surgical team and prepare immediate crossmatched blood transfusion.
6. IF the patient presents with acute severe epigastric pain radiating to the back AND serum lipase elevated three-fold:
   THEN diagnose acute pancreatitis, initiate goal-directed isotonic crystalloid resuscitation, and avoid routine prophylactic antibiotics.
7. IF acute mesenteric ischemia is suspected in an elderly patient with severe abdominal pain out of proportion to physical findings:
   THEN obtain emergent computed tomography mesenteric angiography, administer intravenous heparin, and consult vascular surgery. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF the patient exhibits signs of acute mechanical bowel obstruction with vomiting, distension, AND obstipation:
   THEN insert wide-bore nasogastric tube to low continuous suction, administer intravenous fluids, and obtain abdominal imaging.
9. IF the patient complains of severe distress and pain from acute peritonitis:
   THEN administer titrated intravenous opioid analgesia as formulary-directed as it relieves suffering without obscuring surgical examination.
