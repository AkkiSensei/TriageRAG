## Cerebral Malaria & Complicated Parasitemia Escalation
1. IF the cerebral malaria patient experiences recurrent generalized motor seizures:
   THEN administer intravenous benzodiazepine as formulary-directed and initiate prophylactic antiepileptic therapy.
2. IF hypotension and shock develop in severe malaria (algid malaria) with poor peripheral perfusion:
   THEN administer cautious intravenous crystalloid bolus, evaluate for secondary bacterial sepsis, and start broad-spectrum antibiotics.
3. IF severe lactic acidosis with deep acidotic breathing complicates severe malaria infection:
   THEN optimize oxygen delivery, maintain microvascular perfusion with cautious fluids, and avoid sodium bicarbonate.
4. IF post-artesunate delayed hemolysis develops two to three weeks after completion of intravenous therapy:
   THEN check serial hemoglobin, haptoglobin, and reticulocyte count, and transfuse packed red blood cells if indicated.
5. IF the patient recovers full consciousness and is able to swallow oral medications reliably:
   THEN complete malaria eradication therapy with a full course of oral artemisinin-based combination therapy as formulary-directed.
6. IF concomitant bacterial bloodstream co-infection is suspected with high fever and septic shock:
   THEN administer intravenous third-generation cephalosporin as formulary-directed concurrently with antimalarial therapy.
7. IF severe jaundice with deep scleral icterus develops in the setting of severe hemolysis and hepatic injury:
   THEN monitor liver function tests, coagulation parameters, and avoid hepatotoxic medications.
8. IF refractory coma persists despite clearance of peripheral blood parasitemia:
   THEN perform non-contrast cranial computed tomography scan and evaluate for intracranial hemorrhage or ischemic stroke.
9. IF pregnancy complicates severe malaria presentation in any trimester:
   THEN administer intravenous artesunate as formulary-directed without delay as maternal benefit outweighs potential risks.
