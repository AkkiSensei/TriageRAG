# Emergency Protocol: Exertional & Non-Exertional Heat Stroke (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Exertional & Non-Exertional Heat Stroke
1. IF the patient presents with core body temperature severely elevated AND profound central nervous system dysfunction:
   THEN diagnose acute heat stroke, remove all constrictive clothing, and initiate immediate evaporative or whole-body cold-water immersion cooling.
2. IF the patient experiences persistent motor shivering during rapid evaporative cooling therapy:
   THEN administer intravenous diazepam or midazolam as formulary-directed to suppress shivering and prevent secondary metabolic heat generation.
3. IF the patient exhibits acute circulatory collapse with systolic blood pressure below threshold AND hot dry skin:
   THEN administer rapid intravenous bolus of cooled normal saline as formulary-directed and monitor continuous core body temperature.
4. IF core body temperature declines to target safe threshold during active cooling:
   THEN terminate active aggressive cooling immediately to prevent overshooting hypothermia and transition to passive observation.
5. IF the patient presents with classic non-exertional heat stroke, advanced age, AND chronic diuretic therapy:
   THEN evaluate for severe free-water deficit and electrolyte derangement before administering massive rapid fluid boluses.
6. IF acute rhabdomyolysis is suspected with dark red urine AND serum creatine kinase severely elevated:
   THEN initiate aggressive intravenous crystalloid hydration targeting high hourly urine output and monitor renal function.
7. IF the patient develops acute generalized convulsions during active cooling resuscitation:
   THEN administer intravenous lorazepam as formulary-directed, ensure airway patency, and avoid phenytoin administration.
8. IF the patient exhibits acute agitation, combativeness, or delirium during emergency resuscitation:
   THEN provide calm environment, avoid anticholinergic or phenothiazine restraints, and administer titrated short-acting benzodiazepine.
9. IF the patient is concurrently receiving anticholinergic, neuroleptic, or stimulant medications at home:
   THEN withhold all home psychotropic agents and evaluate for overlapping neuroleptic malignant syndrome or serotonin toxicity.
