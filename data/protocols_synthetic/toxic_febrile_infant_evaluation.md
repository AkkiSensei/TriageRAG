# Emergency Protocol: Toxic Febrile Infant & Invasive Bacterial Infection Escalation (SYNTHETIC)

## Toxic Febrile Infant & Invasive Bacterial Infection Escalation
1. IF the febrile infant exhibits persistent hemodynamic instability with delayed capillary refill and hypotension:
   THEN administer rapid intravenous normal saline fluid bolus as formulary-directed and initiate inotrope infusion if refractory.
2. IF cerebrospinal fluid analysis reveals elevated white blood cells, low glucose, AND gram-positive cocci in pairs:
   THEN diagnose pneumococcal meningitis, administer intravenous vancomycin and cefotaxime as formulary-directed, and consult neurology.
3. IF the febrile infant develops acute respiratory failure requiring mechanical airway protection:
   THEN proceed with endotracheal intubation using age-appropriate uncuffed or microcuffed tube under expert supervision.
4. IF the infant exhibits severe dehydration secondary to fever and refusal to feed:
   THEN administer intravenous fluid replacement as formulary-directed and monitor electrolyte balance closely.
5. IF prolonged fever of five days or longer is accompanied by conjunctival injection, strawberry tongue, AND rash:
   THEN evaluate for Kawasaki disease, obtain echocardiogram, and administer intravenous immunoglobulin as formulary-directed.
6. IF severe acute hyponatremia is discovered on serum electrolyte panel in a febrile infant:
   THEN correct hyponatremia with monitored hypertonic or isotonic fluids as formulary-directed to prevent hyponatremic seizures.
7. IF the infant exhibits persistent hypothermia rather than fever with marked lethargy and hypoglycemia:
   THEN recognise severe sepsis or metabolic crisis, warm infant externally, correct hypoglycemia, and initiate antibiotics.
8. IF laboratory markers reveal markedly elevated C-reactive protein or procalcitonin above high-risk cutoffs:
   THEN escalate level of monitoring, admit to high-dependency unit, and continue parenteral antimicrobial therapy.
9. IF viral pathogen is identified on rapid multiplex testing AND infant remains completely well-appearing:
   THEN re-evaluate necessity of ongoing parenteral antibiotics in consultation with paediatric infectious disease team.
