# Emergency Protocol: Acute Opioid Overdose & Naloxone Titration (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Opioid Overdose & Naloxone Titration
1. IF the patient presents with coma, pin-point pupils, AND severe respiratory depression with bradypnea or apnoea:
   THEN support ventilation with bag-valve-mask high-flow oxygen immediately and administer intravenous or intramuscular naloxone.
2. IF intravenous access is unavailable AND the patient remains severely hypoxemic and apnoeic:
   THEN administer intranasal or intramuscular naloxone as formulary-directed while continuing bag-valve-mask ventilatory assistance.
3. IF spontaneous adequate ventilation fails to resume two to three minutes after initial naloxone administration:
   THEN administer repeat escalated dose of naloxone as formulary-directed until spontaneous respiratory effort is restored.
4. IF the patient awakens abruptly following naloxone with severe agitation, combativeness, AND acute withdrawal symptoms:
   THEN provide verbal reassurance, protect patient and staff safety, and avoid administering additional opioids or sedatives.
5. IF the overdose involves a long-acting opioid formulation such as methadone or extended-release oxycodone:
   THEN observe patient in monitored setting for at least twelve to twenty-four hours to detect renarcotization as naloxone clears.
6. IF the patient develops non-cardiogenic acute pulmonary edema with pink frothy sputum following naloxone reversal:
   THEN apply positive pressure ventilation with supplemental oxygen as formulary-directed and avoid excessive diuretic therapy.
7. IF the patient requires repeated naloxone boluses to maintain adequate minute ventilation:
   THEN initiate continuous intravenous naloxone infusion as formulary-directed with rate titrated to spontaneous respiratory rate.
8. IF the patient has co-ingested central nervous system depressants including benzodiazepines or ethanol:
   THEN maintain supportive mechanical ventilation and avoid flumazenil as it precipitates intractable refractory seizures.
9. IF the patient regains normal alertness, normal respiratory rate, AND requests discharge against medical advice within one hour:
   THEN strongly advise continued observation, warn of renarcotization risk, and provide take-home naloxone kit with instructions.
