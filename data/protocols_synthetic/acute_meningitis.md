# Emergency Protocol: Acute Bacterial Meningitis & Encephalitis (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Bacterial Meningitis & Encephalitis
1. IF the patient presents with fever, severe headache, neck stiffness, AND altered mental status:
   THEN initiate droplet transmission precautions, obtain emergent blood cultures, and administer dexamethasone with empiric third-generation cephalosporin and vancomycin as formulary-directed.
2. IF the patient exhibits signs of space-occupying lesion, focal neurological deficit, or new seizure prior to planned lumbar puncture:
   THEN obtain emergent non-contrast cranial computed tomography scan before lumbar puncture while administering empiric antimicrobial therapy without delay.
3. IF the patient with suspected bacterial meningitis exhibits signs of respiratory depression, severe hypoxia, AND deteriorating consciousness:
   THEN administer high-flow supplemental oxygen via non-rebreather mask and prepare for emergent airway protection.
4. IF the patient is aged over fifty years or immunocompromised presenting with acute bacterial meningitis:
   THEN add intravenous ampicillin as formulary-directed to empiric antimicrobial regimen to provide antimicrobial coverage for Listeria monocytogenes.
5. IF the patient develops sudden petechial or purpuric cutaneous rash with rapid progression:
   THEN diagnose fulminant meningococcemia, establish wide-bore vascular access, and administer stress-dose steroids and fluid resuscitation as formulary-directed.
6. IF cerebrospinal fluid analysis reveals opening pressure markedly elevated with turbid appearance:
   THEN elevate head of bed to thirty degrees, avoid jugular compression, and evaluate for hyperosmolar therapy if signs of herniation appear.
7. IF the patient experiences generalized motor seizure during evaluation of suspected meningitis:
   THEN administer intravenous lorazepam as formulary-directed and initiate levetiracetam loading infusion for seizure prophylaxis. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF the patient has a documented severe cephalosporin allergy with anaphylaxis history:
   THEN substitute intravenous chloramphenicol or meropenem as formulary-directed in immediate consultation with infectious disease specialist.
9. IF the patient exhibits severe persistent photophobia and pain despite initial antibiotic infusion:
   THEN administer intravenous paracetamol or multimodal non-opioid analgesia as formulary-directed in a quiet darkened room.
