2. IF the patient exhibits plasma leakage with rising hematocrit, pleural effusion, OR clinical ascites:
   THEN initiate titrated intravenous crystalloid fluid infusion as formulary-directed matching hourly fluid loss.
3. IF the patient enters dengue shock syndrome with narrow pulse pressure, tachycardia, AND cold extremities:
   THEN administer rapid intravenous isotonic crystalloid bolus as formulary-directed over thirty minutes and re-assess.
4. IF shock persists in severe dengue despite initial crystalloid fluid boluses AND hematocrit remains high:
   THEN administer intravenous colloid solution as formulary-directed over thirty to sixty minutes under close supervision.
5. IF shock persists in severe dengue BUT hematocrit drops precipitously without clinical fluid overload:
   THEN suspect severe occult internal bleeding, order emergency crossmatched whole blood or packed red blood cells, and transfuse.
6. IF severe thrombocytopenia is documented in a stable dengue patient WITHOUT any clinical hemorrhage:
   THEN withhold prophylactic platelet transfusions as they do not prevent bleeding and can cause fluid overload.
7. IF the patient exhibits signs of clinical fluid overload with tachypnea, crackles, AND large pleural effusions:
   THEN reduce or stop intravenous fluids immediately and administer intravenous loop diuretic if hemodynamically stable. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.

   THEN provide supportive care, monitor coagulation and blood glucose, and avoid hepatotoxic analgesics.
9. IF the patient complains of severe retro-orbital headache, myalgias, and high fever in dengue:
   THEN administer oral paracetamol as formulary-directed and strictly avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and aspirin.
