# Emergency Protocol: Acute Tachyarrhythmia & Bradyarrhythmia (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Tachyarrhythmia & Bradyarrhythmia
1. IF the patient exhibits severe bradycardia with heart rate below threshold AND hypotension or altered mental status:
   THEN administer intravenous atropine as formulary-directed while attaching transcutaneous pacing pads.
2. IF atropine is ineffective for symptomatic bradycardia AND hemodynamic instability persists:
   THEN initiate transcutaneous electrical cardiac pacing and titrate intravenous dopamine or epinephrine infusion as formulary-directed.
3. IF the patient exhibits narrow-complex regular tachycardia with heart rate markedly elevated AND is hemodynamically stable:
   THEN perform non-invasive vagal maneuvers such as the modified Valsalva maneuver with leg elevation.
4. IF vagal maneuvers fail to terminate stable regular narrow-complex supraventricular tachycardia:
   THEN administer rapid intravenous adenosine as formulary-directed followed by immediate rapid saline flush under continuous rhythm recording.
5. IF the patient exhibits rapid atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response AND is hemodynamically stable:
   THEN administer intravenous diltiazem or metoprolol as formulary-directed to achieve ventricular rate control.
6. IF the patient has a known history of Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome and pre-excited atrial fibrillation:
   THEN withhold all atrioventricular nodal blocking agents including adenosine, beta-blockers, and calcium-channel blockers.
7. IF the patient exhibits wide-complex regular tachycardia of uncertain origin AND is hemodynamically stable:
   THEN treat as ventricular tachycardia and administer intravenous amiodarone or procainamide as formulary-directed.
8. IF any tachyarrhythmia produces hemodynamic instability including hypotension, chest pain, or altered consciousness:
   THEN perform immediate synchronized electrical cardioversion under procedural sedation as formulary-directed.
9. IF polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with prolonged baseline QT interval (torsades de pointes) is documented:
   THEN administer intravenous magnesium sulfate as formulary-directed and discontinue all potential QT-prolonging medications.
