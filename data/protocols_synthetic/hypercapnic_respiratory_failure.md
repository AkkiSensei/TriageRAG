## Hypercapnic Respiratory Failure & Non-Invasive Ventilation Escalation
1. IF the patient on non-invasive positive pressure ventilation exhibits worsening respiratory acidosis at one-hour check:
   THEN increase inspiratory positive airway pressure by two to three centimeters of water as formulary-directed and re-check gas.
2. IF the patient on non-invasive ventilation develops severe facial skin breakdown or gastric distension:
   THEN adjust mask interface cushion, insert venting nasogastric tube, and optimize humidification.
3. IF concurrent acute pulmonary embolism is suspected in an acute exacerbation with refractory hypoxemia without sputum:
   THEN obtain computed tomography pulmonary angiography or ventilation-perfusion scan and initiate anticoagulation if confirmed.
4. IF acute cor pulmonale with peripheral edema, elevated jugular venous pressure, AND hepatic congestion is present:
   THEN cautiously optimize fluid balance, avoid over-diuresis that worsens renal perfusion, and treat hypoxemia.
5. IF severe unmanageable agitation impairs tolerance of non-invasive positive pressure mask interface:
   THEN provide bedside clinician reassurance and consider low-dose intravenous dexmedetomidine as formulary-directed.
6. IF the mechanically ventilated chronic obstructive pulmonary disease patient is being prepared for liberation:
   THEN perform spontaneous breathing trial with low pressure support and transition directly to non-invasive ventilation upon extubation.
7. IF the patient develops significant cardiac arrhythmia associated with frequent beta-agonist administration and hypokalemia:
   THEN monitor serum potassium and magnesium, correct deficits, and reduce beta-agonist frequency where clinically feasible.
8. IF secondary polycythemia with hematocrit severely elevated is noted in chronic hypoxemic lung disease:
   THEN optimize continuous long-term oxygen therapy and ensure adequate oral and intravenous hydration.
9. IF the patient achieves clinical stability with normalized pH and safe oxygenation on room air or baseline oxygen:
   THEN transition from acute nebulisation to metered-dose inhalers with spacer device and provide action plan.
