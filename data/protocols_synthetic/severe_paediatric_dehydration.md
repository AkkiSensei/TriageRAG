## Refractory Paediatric Dehydration & Electrolyte Disturbance
1. IF severe hypokalemia with muscle weakness or electrocardiographic U-waves develops during rehydration:
   THEN add potassium chloride to maintenance intravenous fluids as formulary-directed once adequate urine output confirmed.
2. IF the child exhibits paralytic ileus, abdominal distension, AND severe potassium depletion:
   THEN withhold oral rehydration solution, insert nasogastric tube on low suction, and correct potassium intravenously.
3. IF the infant develops generalized convulsions during rapid rehydration for severe hypernatremic dehydration:
   THEN suspect acute cerebral edema, administer intravenous hypertonic saline or mannitol as formulary-directed, and slow infusion.
4. IF the child has severe acute malnutrition (marasmus or kwashiorkor) with signs of dehydration:
   THEN withhold standard rapid intravenous hydration and use low-sodium rehydration solution for malnutrition as formulary-directed.
5. IF blood in stool is reported with high fever, tenesmus, and toxicity in acute diarrhea:
   THEN diagnose acute bacillary dysentery, maintain hydration, and administer targeted oral antimicrobial therapy as formulary-directed.
6. IF the child remains persistently anuric after adequate fluid volume replacement has been completed:
   THEN evaluate for acute tubular necrosis or hemolytic uremic syndrome, avoid potassium-containing fluids, and check renal panel.
7. IF severe metabolic acidosis with deep acidotic respirations persists despite dehydration correction:
   THEN evaluate for underlying inborn error of metabolism, severe sepsis, or diabetic ketoacidosis.
8. IF hypocalcemia with carpopedal spasm or jitteriness develops during correction of severe dehydration:
   THEN administer intravenous calcium gluconate as formulary-directed under continuous cardiac rhythm monitoring.
9. IF the infant displays signs of fluid overload including periorbital edema, tachypnea, and pulmonary crackles:
   THEN stop intravenous rehydration fluids immediately, restrict fluid intake, and consult paediatric specialist.
