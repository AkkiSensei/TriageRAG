# Emergency Protocol: Adult Cardiac Arrest Resuscitation (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Adult Cardiac Arrest Resuscitation
1. IF the adult patient is unresponsive, apnoeic or only gasping, AND no carotid pulse is palpable within ten seconds:
   THEN initiate immediate high-quality chest compressions and attach defibrillator pads without interruption.
2. IF the cardiac rhythm is identified as ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia:
   THEN deliver an immediate unsynchronized defibrillator shock and immediately resume chest compressions for two minutes.
3. IF the cardiac rhythm is identified as asystole or pulseless electrical activity:
   THEN continue high-quality chest compressions, secure vascular access, and administer intravenous epinephrine as formulary-directed.
4. IF shockable rhythm persists after the second defibrillation attempt and continuous compressions:
   THEN administer intravenous epinephrine and prepare intravenous amiodarone or lidocaine as formulary-directed.
5. IF the patient exhibits return of spontaneous circulation with palpable pulse AND measurable blood pressure:
   THEN initiate targeted post-cardiac arrest temperature management, obtain immediate 12-lead electrocardiogram, and assess for systemic hypoperfusion.
6. IF post-cardiac arrest hypotension with systolic blood pressure below target is documented:
   THEN initiate intravenous crystalloid bolus and titrate intravenous norepinephrine or dopamine as formulary-directed.
7. IF severe persistent hypoxemia is noted during resuscitation despite bag-valve-mask ventilation:
   THEN insert advanced supraglottic airway or endotracheal tube with waveform capnography confirmation. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF hyperkalemia or renal failure is suspected as the primary cause of cardiac arrest:
   THEN administer intravenous calcium gluconate and intravenous sodium bicarbonate as formulary-directed.
9. IF acute massive pulmonary embolism is suspected as the underlying trigger of refractory arrest:
   THEN consider emergent systemic thrombolysis as formulary-directed in consultation with senior resuscitation staff.
