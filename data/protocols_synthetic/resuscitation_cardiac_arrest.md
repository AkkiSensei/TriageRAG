## Post-Cardiac Arrest Care & Refractory Resuscitation
1. IF return of spontaneous circulation is achieved AND the patient remains comatose without purposeful response:
   THEN avoid hyperthermia, maintain normothermia, and consult neurointensive care for neuroprognostication pathway.
2. IF 12-lead electrocardiogram reveals ST-segment elevation following return of spontaneous circulation:
   THEN transfer immediately to cardiac catheterisation suite for emergent percutaneous coronary angiography.
3. IF refractory ventricular fibrillation continues after three shocks, epinephrine, and antiarrhythmic therapy:
   THEN evaluate for reversible causes including severe hypothermia, acute coronary occlusion, and electrolyte derangement.
4. IF waveform capnography displays sudden sustained increase in end-tidal carbon dioxide during compressions:
   THEN pause chest compressions briefly to verify return of spontaneous circulation via pulse and rhythm check.
5. IF prolonged resuscitation exceeds twenty minutes in a young patient with potentially reversible etiology:
   THEN activate extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation protocol where local resources permit.
6. IF persistent severe arterial hypotension is unresponsive to high-dose vasopressor infusion:
   THEN insert arterial line, evaluate central venous saturation, and consider second-line vasopressin as formulary-directed.
7. IF acute tension pneumothorax is suspected during resuscitation with asymmetric chest expansion:
   THEN perform immediate needle decompression in the second intercostal space at the midclavicular line.
8. IF profound hypothermia with core temperature below normal is present in cardiac arrest:
   THEN continue active internal rewarming and withhold repeated defibrillation until core temperature reaches target threshold.
9. IF the patient develops post-arrest myoclonus or clinical status epilepticus:
   THEN administer intravenous levetiracetam or valproate as formulary-directed and initiate continuous electroencephalography.
