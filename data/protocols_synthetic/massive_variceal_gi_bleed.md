## Massive Variceal Hemorrhage & Balloon Tamponade Escalation
1. IF massive variceal hemorrhage continues unabated despite vasoactive infusions and emergency endoscopic therapy:
   THEN insert Sengstaken-Blakemore or Minnesota balloon tamponade tube as formulary-directed as temporary salvage bridge.
2. IF balloon tamponade tube is deployed for refractory gastrointestinal bleeding:
   THEN ensure prior endotracheal intubation, verify gastric balloon position radiographically, and limit inflation to twenty-four hours.
3. IF the patient is in persistent shock with massive transfusion requirement exceeding four units of blood:
   THEN activate institutional massive transfusion protocol with balanced one-to-one-to-one ratio of blood, plasma, and platelets.
4. IF variceal hemorrhage remains uncontrolled after balloon tamponade and endoscopic failure:
   THEN request emergency interventional radiology consultation for salvage transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt.
5. IF non-variceal peptic ulcer bleeding is refractory to endoscopic dual therapy with clipping and thermocoagulation:
   THEN consult interventional radiology for transcatheter arterial embolisation or general surgery for operative intervention.
6. IF severe thrombocytopenia with platelet count critically depressed complicates massive gastrointestinal hemorrhage:
   THEN administer therapeutic platelet transfusions as formulary-directed targeting safe hemostatic platelet threshold.
7. IF the patient develops severe hepatic encephalopathy during resuscitation for upper gastrointestinal bleeding:
   THEN initiate oral or nasogastric lactulose as formulary-directed to evacuate blood from gut and lower ammonia burden.
8. IF acute hypocalcemia develops during massive transfusion secondary to citrate anticoagulant binding:
   THEN administer intravenous calcium gluconate or calcium chloride as formulary-directed with ionized calcium monitoring.
9. IF the patient achieves hemostasis and hemodynamic stability with hemoglobin sustained within safe restrictive target:
   THEN maintain restrictive transfusion threshold, avoid over-transfusion, and transition to oral maintenance proton pump inhibitors.
