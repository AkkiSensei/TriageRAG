## Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar Coma & Electrolyte Shift Management
1. IF the patient with hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state presents with profound dehydration AND hypovolemic shock:
   THEN administer isotonic crystalloid resuscitation until hemodynamic stability is restored before correcting hypertonicity.
2. IF corrected serum sodium is elevated or normal after initial fluid resuscitation in hyperosmolar state:
   THEN switch maintenance fluids to half-normal saline as formulary-directed to replace free water deficit gradually.
3. IF plasma glucose fails to decline by expected hourly decrement despite continuous insulin infusion:
   THEN verify intravenous line patency, double insulin infusion rate as formulary-directed, and re-evaluate hydration status.
4. IF severe hypophosphatemia develops during ketoacidosis resolution with muscle weakness or respiratory depression:
   THEN administer intravenous potassium or sodium phosphate as formulary-directed with continuous calcium monitoring.
5. IF the patient exhibits signs of systemic infection or sepsis triggering hyperglycemic emergency:
   THEN obtain diagnostic blood, urine, and sputum cultures and initiate broad-spectrum intravenous antimicrobial therapy.
6. IF the patient is anuric or displays acute oliguric kidney injury during resuscitation:
   THEN adjust potassium replacement rate cautiously, place indwelling catheter, and consult nephrology service.
7. IF diabetic ketoacidosis resolves with closed anion gap, normal bicarbonate, AND patient is alert and hungry:
   THEN administer subcutaneous basal insulin as formulary-directed two hours prior to stopping intravenous insulin infusion.
8. IF acute vascular thrombosis or thromboembolic complication occurs in the setting of severe hyperviscosity:
   THEN administer therapeutic anticoagulation as formulary-directed and enforce mechanical and chemical thromboprophylaxis.
9. IF persistent vomiting and gastric distension are present in diabetic gastroparesis during resuscitation:
   THEN insert nasogastric tube to decompress stomach, prevent aspiration, and administer intravenous antiemetics as formulary-directed.
