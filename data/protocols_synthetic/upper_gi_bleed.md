# Emergency Protocol: Acute Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage
1. IF the patient presents with acute hematemesis, coffee-ground emesis, melena, AND hemodynamic instability:
   THEN establish two large-bore peripheral intravenous lines, initiate crystalloid infusion, and crossmatch packed red blood cells.
2. IF the patient exhibits signs of hemorrhagic shock with systolic blood pressure below threshold AND tachycardia:
   THEN administer emergency uncrossed or type-specific blood transfusion as formulary-directed and activate massive transfusion protocol.
3. IF the patient has a known history of chronic liver disease, cirrhosis, OR portal hypertension:
   THEN initiate intravenous octreotide or terlipressin infusion as formulary-directed and administer prophylactic ceftriaxone.
4. IF acute upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage is suspected secondary to peptic ulcer disease:
   THEN administer high-dose intravenous proton pump inhibitor omeprazole or pantoprazole as formulary-directed.
5. IF the patient is actively receiving oral anticoagulant therapy with warfarin AND exhibits severe ongoing hemorrhage:
   THEN administer four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate and intravenous vitamin K as formulary-directed for rapid reversal.
6. IF the patient is receiving direct oral anticoagulants such as dabigatran, apixaban, OR rivaroxaban:
   THEN administer specific reversal agent idarucizumab or andexanet alfa as formulary-directed if life-threatening bleeding persists.
7. IF massive ongoing hematemesis produces airway compromise or profound aspiration risk:
   THEN perform rapid sequence endotracheal intubation by experienced airway specialist to secure and protect airway.
8. IF the patient achieves hemodynamic stabilization following resuscitation AND active bleeding is suspected:
   THEN consult gastroenterology for emergent diagnostic and therapeutic upper gastrointestinal endoscopy within twelve hours.
9. IF nasogastric tube placement is considered for diagnostic evaluation in suspected upper gastrointestinal bleed:
   THEN avoid routine nasogastric lavage as it does not improve clinical outcomes and causes patient discomfort.
