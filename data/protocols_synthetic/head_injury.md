## Acute Severe Traumatic Brain Injury
1. IF the trauma patient presents with Glasgow Coma Scale score below nine following blunt head injury:
   THEN perform rapid sequence endotracheal intubation to protect airway, prevent secondary hypoxia, and stabilize cervical spine.
2. IF the head injury patient exhibits systemic arterial hypotension with systolic blood pressure below target threshold:
   THEN administer intravenous isotonic crystalloid or blood boluses as formulary-directed to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure.
3. IF the patient exhibits signs of acute transtentorial herniation with unilateral dilated unreactive pupil AND decerebrate posturing:
   THEN administer intravenous hypertonic saline or mannitol as formulary-directed, induce brief hyperventilation, and alert neurosurgery.
4. IF non-contrast cranial computed tomography reveals acute epidural hematoma with midline shift greater than five millimeters:
   THEN consult neurosurgery STAT for emergency craniotomy and surgical hematoma evacuation.
5. IF the patient exhibits severe acute subdural hematoma with significant mass effect and neurological deterioration:
   THEN prepare immediately for emergency neurosurgical decompression and reverse any pre-injury anticoagulant medications.
6. IF the head-injured patient develops post-traumatic seizure activity or status epilepticus:
   THEN administer intravenous lorazepam followed by intravenous levetiracetam or fosphenytoin as formulary-directed.
7. IF prophylactic antiepileptic therapy is considered for severe traumatic brain injury with depressed skull fracture or hemorrhage:
   THEN initiate intravenous levetiracetam as formulary-directed for seven days to prevent early post-traumatic seizures. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF the patient exhibits open or depressed cranial vault fracture with cerebrospinal fluid leak or brain tissue exposure:
   THEN administer intravenous prophylactic broad-spectrum antibiotics, avoid wound probing, and obtain urgent neurosurgical repair.
9. IF arterial blood gas analysis reveals hypoxemia with oxygen saturation below target or severe hypercapnia:
   THEN adjust mechanical ventilator to maintain normal arterial oxygenation and strictly avoid prolonged prophylactic hyperventilation.
