# Emergency Protocol: Acute Paracetamol Overdose & Acetylcysteine Protocol (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Paracetamol Overdose & Acetylcysteine Protocol
1. IF the patient presents within four hours of acute paracetamol ingestion exceeding toxic threshold:
   THEN administer oral activated charcoal as formulary-directed if the patient is fully alert and airway is protected.
2. IF a timed serum paracetamol concentration measured four hours or later plots above the treatment nomogram nomogram line:
   THEN initiate intravenous acetylcysteine infusion as formulary-directed without delay to prevent acute hepatotoxicity.
3. IF the patient presents more than eight hours post-ingestion OR the exact ingestion time is unknown and toxic dose suspected:
   THEN initiate intravenous acetylcysteine infusion as formulary-directed immediately before serum paracetamol levels return.
4. IF the patient develops a non-immunologic anaphylactoid reaction to acetylcysteine with flushing, rash, OR mild wheezing:
   THEN pause infusion briefly, administer oral or intravenous antihistamine as formulary-directed, and resume at slower rate.
5. IF severe systemic anaphylaxis with hypotension or stridor occurs during acetylcysteine administration:
   THEN stop infusion immediately, administer intramuscular epinephrine as formulary-directed, and switch to oral acetylcysteine if feasible.
6. IF the patient has ingested a modified-release or extended-release paracetamol formulation:
   THEN obtain serial paracetamol levels at four-hour intervals, extend acetylcysteine therapy, and consult clinical toxicology.
7. IF the patient presents with repeated supratherapeutic ingestions over twenty-four to forty-eight hours with elevated transaminases:
   THEN initiate full-course intravenous acetylcysteine infusion as formulary-directed regardless of single-level nomogram lines.
8. IF the initial acetylcysteine regimen is complete BUT serum paracetamol remains detectable OR transaminases remain elevated:
   THEN continue intravenous acetylcysteine maintenance infusion as formulary-directed until paracetamol is undetectable and enzymes improve.
9. IF the patient presents within two hours of co-ingestion of massive paracetamol and sedative medication:
   THEN perform cautious gastric lavage only if airway is definitively protected by endotracheal intubation.
