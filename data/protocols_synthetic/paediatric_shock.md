# Emergency Protocol: Paediatric Shock Initial Resuscitation (SYNTHETIC)

## Paediatric Shock Initial Resuscitation
1. IF the paediatric patient exhibits tachycardia, prolonged capillary refill, AND cool extremities with normal blood pressure:
   THEN recognise compensated shock, apply high-flow oxygen, establish vascular access, and prepare crystalloid bolus.
2. IF the paediatric patient exhibits tachycardia, weak pulses, AND systolic blood pressure below age-adjusted minimum:
   THEN recognise decompensated shock, administer rapid intravenous or intraosseous fluid bolus as formulary-directed, and re-assess.
3. IF signs of poor perfusion persist after initial crystalloid fluid bolus in paediatric shock:
   THEN administer repeat crystalloid fluid bolus as formulary-directed unless hepatomegaly or crackles develop.
4. IF hepatomegaly, new pulmonary crackles, or facial edema develop during fluid resuscitation:
   THEN stop fluid administration immediately, recognize fluid overload, and prepare inotrope infusion.
5. IF the infant or child remains in cold shock with prolonged capillary refill despite fluid resuscitation:
   THEN initiate intravenous epinephrine infusion as formulary-directed via peripheral or intraosseous access.
6. IF the infant or child exhibits warm shock with flash capillary refill, wide pulse pressure, and bounding pulses:
   THEN initiate intravenous norepinephrine infusion as formulary-directed to restore systemic vascular tone and treat peripheral hypoperfusion.
7. IF the infant has a known or suspected ductal-dependent congenital heart defect presenting with cyanosis and shock:
   THEN initiate continuous intravenous prostaglandin E1 alprostadil as formulary-directed to reopen the ductus arteriosus. Assess for clinical signs of end-organ hypoperfusion.
8. IF severe acute blood loss or traumatic hemorrhagic shock is identified in a paediatric patient:
   THEN administer emergency uncrossed or type-specific packed red blood cells as formulary-directed.
9. IF capillary blood glucose is below age-appropriate threshold in an infant or child in shock:
   THEN administer intravenous or intraosseous dextrose bolus as formulary-directed and re-check glucose within fifteen minutes.
