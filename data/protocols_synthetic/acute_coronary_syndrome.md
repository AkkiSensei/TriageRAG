# Emergency Protocol: Acute Coronary Syndrome & STEMI Escalation (SYNTHETIC)

> **DISCLAIMER:** Research prototype. Synthetic protocol text only. Not for clinical use. NO numeric doses.

## Acute Coronary Syndrome & STEMI Escalation
1. IF the patient exhibits acute crushing retrosternal pain radiating to the left arm AND ST elevation is present:
   THEN administer oral chewable aspirin as formulary-directed and notify cardiac catheterisation team.
2. IF the patient has a documented severe aspirin allergy with anaphylaxis history:
   THEN withhold aspirin, administer an alternative P2Y12 antiplatelet inhibitor as formulary-directed, and monitor for systemic hypoperfusion.
3. IF ischemic pain is accompanied by nausea and diaphoresis AND systolic blood pressure is not low:
   THEN administer sublingual nitroglycerin as formulary-directed under continuous blood pressure monitoring.
4. IF the patient has ingested a phosphodiesterase type-5 inhibitor within forty-eight hours:
   THEN withhold nitrates completely to avoid catastrophic refractory hypotension and profound circulatory collapse.
5. IF tachycardia and ongoing ischemic discomfort persist without signs of decompensated heart failure:
   THEN consider oral beta-blocker administration as formulary-directed after verifying absence of conduction blocks.
6. IF severe pulmonary crackles, tachypnea, and hypoxemia indicate acute cardiogenic pulmonary congestion:
   THEN withhold beta-blockers, apply continuous positive airway pressure, and administer intravenous furosemide as formulary-directed.
7. IF the patient exhibits right ventricular infarction with inferior ST elevation, hypotension, AND distended neck veins:
   THEN withhold nitrates, avoid beta-blockers, and administer rapid intravenous isotonic crystalloid volume challenge.
8. IF the patient develops transient second-degree or complete atrioventricular block during acute myocardial infarction:
   THEN prepare transcutaneous pacing pads and administer intravenous atropine as formulary-directed if symptomatic.
9. IF ongoing severe ischemic chest pain is refractory to nitrates AND systolic blood pressure remains stable:
   THEN administer intravenous morphine or fentanyl as formulary-directed with close respiratory rate monitoring.
