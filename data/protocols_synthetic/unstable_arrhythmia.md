## Unstable Arrhythmia & Refractory Rate Control
1. IF the patient exhibits wide-complex tachycardia with sudden loss of consciousness AND pulselessness:
   THEN switch defibrillator to asynchronous mode immediately, deliver an unsynchronized high-energy shock, and begin compressions.
2. IF refractory supraventricular tachycardia fails to terminate after repeat adenosine administrations:
   THEN administer intravenous verapamil or diltiazem as formulary-directed if systolic blood pressure and ventricular function are normal.
3. IF acute atrial flutter is identified with rapid ventricular conduction and symptomatic palpitations:
   THEN perform synchronized electrical cardioversion starting at low energy level or initiate rate control as formulary-directed.
4. IF transcutaneous pacing fails to capture mechanically despite maximal current output:
   THEN prepare immediately for emergency transvenous pacing wire insertion under fluoroscopic or ultrasound guidance.
5. IF bradycardia is secondary to acute digoxin toxicity with hyperkalemia AND ventricular ectopy:
   THEN administer digoxin-specific antibody fragments as formulary-directed and withhold intravenous calcium formulations.
6. IF calcium-channel blocker or beta-blocker overdose is identified as the cause of refractory bradycardia and shock:
   THEN initiate high-dose insulin euglycemic therapy and administer intravenous calcium gluconate as formulary-directed.
7. IF severe hyperkalemia with peaked T waves, widened QRS, and sine-wave appearance is identified:
   THEN administer immediate intravenous calcium chloride or gluconate as formulary-directed for myocardial membrane stabilization.
8. IF electrical cardioversion attempts result in immediate reversion to unstable ventricular tachycardia:
   THEN administer repeat intravenous antiarrhythmic bolus, correct hypokalemia, and consider emergent coronary revascularization.
9. IF the patient exhibits persistent asymptomatic sinus bradycardia without hemodynamic compromise:
   THEN monitor continuous telemetry, identify offending home medications, and avoid unnecessary invasive pacing interventions.
